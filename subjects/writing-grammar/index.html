Mechanics

Grammar rules students miss most

RuleExample
Its vs. it'sIts = possessive ("the dog wagged its tail"). It's = "it is" ("it's raining")
Their / there / they'reTheir = possessive, there = place, they're = "they are"
Subject-verb agreementA singular subject takes a singular verb even if other words come between them: "The list of items is long."
Comma spliceWrong: "I studied all night, I still failed." Fix with a period, semicolon, or conjunction: "I studied all night, but I still failed."
Who vs. whomWho is a subject ("who called?"), whom is an object ("to whom it may concern")
Affect vs. effectAffect is usually a verb ("this will affect me"), effect is usually a noun ("the effect was clear")
Parallel structureKeep list items in the same grammatical form: "I like reading, writing, and to swim" → "I like reading, writing, and swimming."

Templates

Essay structures by type

Persuasive essay
Thesis stating your position → 2-3 body paragraphs each with a reason and evidence → a paragraph addressing the counterargument → conclusion restating your position.
Analytical essay
Thesis making a specific claim about the text or topic → body paragraphs each proving one part of the claim with evidence → conclusion connecting back to significance.
Narrative essay
A hook that drops the reader into a moment → rising action building tension → a turning point or realization → a reflection on what it meant.
Five-paragraph essay (default)
Intro with thesis → three body paragraphs, one per supporting point → conclusion. A safe default structure for most timed writing.

Before You Submit

Revision checklist

Read it out loud
Awkward sentences and missing words are far easier to catch by ear than by eye.
Check that every paragraph proves the thesis
If a paragraph doesn't clearly support your central argument, cut it or rewrite it so it does.
Cut filler words
Look for "very," "really," "in order to," and "the fact that" - almost all of them can be deleted without losing meaning.
Check your first and last sentence
These get read the most carefully - make sure the opening earns attention and the closing leaves an actual point, not just a summary.
