Memorization

How to build vocabulary that sticks

Learn words in context, not isolation
A word attached to a sentence or image is far easier to recall than a word on its own in a list.
Use spaced repetition
Review new words at increasing intervals (1 day, 3 days, a week) rather than cramming - it's the single most effective vocabulary technique. The Word List Driller automates this.
Prioritize high-frequency words first
The most common 1,000 words in any language cover the vast majority of everyday conversation - learn those before rare vocabulary.
Group by theme
Learning "kitchen words" or "travel words" together builds a usable cluster of vocabulary faster than a random alphabetical list.

Grammar

Verb conjugation basics

ConceptWhat it means
ConjugationChanging a verb's form to match its subject (I, you, he/she, we, they) and tense
Regular verbsFollow a predictable pattern based on their ending (e.g., Spanish -ar, -er, -ir verbs)
Irregular verbsDon't follow the standard pattern and must be memorized individually - usually the most common verbs (to be, to go, to have)
TenseWhen the action happens - past, present, future
MoodThe speaker's attitude toward the action - indicative (fact), subjunctive (doubt/wish), imperative (command)

Strategy: master the present tense of regular verbs first, then the 10-15 most common irregular verbs - that combination unlocks the majority of everyday sentences.

Practice

Building daily immersion habits

Switch one device to the target language
Your phone's system language forces small reps of reading throughout the day without extra study time.
Speak out loud, even alone
Producing the language, not just recognizing it, builds a different and necessary set of skills.
Consume media just above your level
Shows, songs, or articles slightly harder than what's comfortable stretch your vocabulary without being overwhelming.

Common Mistakes

Where students go wrong in language learning

Patterns we see again and again in student work. Each one is fixable once you know to look for it.

Learning words in isolation
A vocabulary list of single words maps poorly onto real sentences. Learn each word with a short example phrase (not "to go" but "I am going to school"), and drill the phrase. It carries the grammar with it and is far easier to recall in conversation.
Only recognizing, never producing
Reading a word list and nodding along feels like knowing. The test of knowing a word is producing it, unprompted, in the target language. Drill from English into the target language at least as often as the reverse. The Word List Driller does both directions.
Memorizing conjugation tables as tables
A conjugation table is a reference, not a study object. You learn conjugations by using them: write ten sentences with the verb in different persons, say them out loud, then do it again tomorrow. The pattern goes in through the hand and the mouth, not the eyes.
Avoiding speaking until "ready"
Nobody is ever ready. Speaking badly is the only path to speaking well, and the fear of mistakes is the single biggest obstacle most students face. Talk to yourself, narrate what you are doing, record a voice memo. Ten minutes of terrible speaking a day beats an hour of silent review.
Cramming before tests and then stopping
Language is the subject where spacing matters most, because everything is cumulative. Fifteen minutes every day beats two hours on Sunday, by a wide margin, and the difference compounds over a year. See the spaced repetition guide.
